# HMSI Live Volunteer Support Peer-Coaching Checklist
## For Team Leads and Authorized Support Owners

**Purpose:** Help team leads coach one another while real volunteer support conversations are being conducted, without turning coaching into surveillance, performance scoring, or unnecessary collection of sensitive information.  
**Use:** Complete with the participating team lead’s knowledge and consent. Use the minimum necessary details and record coaching observations separately from the volunteer’s support record.  
**Status:** Reusable operational checklist; adapt names, service levels, and confidential routes before launch.

> **Safety boundary:** A peer coach observes communication quality and process adherence. The coach must not investigate safeguarding concerns, diagnose wellbeing or medical conditions, request passwords or access tokens, copy sensitive case details, or override the designated confidential process. If a real confidential concern emerges, pause coaching and follow the approved route.

---

## 1. Coaching Agreement

Before the live check-in, the coach and team lead should agree the purpose, scope, channel, and stop conditions. The volunteer should know, where appropriate, that a support owner may be accompanied for learning and quality improvement. Do not record audio, video, screen captures, or transcripts unless a separate approved consent and governance process exists.

|---|---|

The coach should observe, not take over. If the conversation becomes unsafe, the coach may use the agreed stop signal or contact the authorized lead according to procedure.

## 4. Trigger and Escalation Check

The peer coach should not classify a volunteer or determine the merits of a disclosure. The coach checks whether the team lead recognized the process boundary and used the right route.

| Trigger observed | Team-lead response | Coach check |
|---|---|---|
| A low score or task blocker without a safety concern | Offer proportionate routine support, adjustment, or clarification. | Was one practical next step agreed without overreacting? |
| A capacity or pause request | Respect the choice and adjust or pause work without penalty. | Was personal justification avoided? |
| Portal or room access problem | Provide safe technical support without requesting credentials or bypassing role rules. | Were authorization and privacy preserved? |
| Coercive or punitive communication concern | Acknowledge impact, correct the message, and restore voluntary choice. | Did the lead avoid defending intent as the first response? |
| Safety, safeguarding, privacy, security, harassment, retaliation, or serious wellbeing concern | Stop routine questioning and use the confidential route. | Was ordinary analysis stopped promptly? |
| Unclear or mixed evidence | Label the issue as monitoring or insufficient evidence. | Did the lead avoid inventing facts or diagnosing the volunteer? |
| System outage or unavailable workflow | Give a bounded status update and use the approved manual fallback. | Were duplicate submissions prevented and reconciliation planned? |

If the coach is unsure whether a concern is confidential, treat the uncertainty cautiously and consult the authorized lead.

## 5. Post-Conversation Debrief

The debrief should occur privately and as soon as practical. Allow the team lead to reflect first, then share evidence-based coaching. Do not discuss the volunteer’s personal details unless they are essential to a confidential process and the coach is authorized to know them.

| What action, owner, date, and task state were agreed? | [ ] |

### Peer coach feedback

Start with two specific strengths, then offer one improvement. Use observable language:

> “You asked permission to continue and confirmed that the volunteer could pause. That preserved choice. One improvement would be to state the owner and date before closing so the next step is easier to remember.”

Avoid personality judgments such as “You were empathetic,” “You sounded weak,” or “You need to be tougher.” Describe the behavior, its effect, and the next practice step.

## 7. Immediate Stop and Escalation Rules

Stop the coaching exercise or observation immediately if a participant discloses a real incident, asks for a confidential case investigation, requests a password or token, becomes distressed, or begins sharing sensitive details in a general channel. Use a calm reset:

> “Let us pause here. We do not need the details in this coaching record. This needs to use the designated confidential HMSI route, and we will share only the minimum necessary information.”

Contact the authorized safeguarding, privacy, security, or wellbeing lead according to the approved route. Do not promise absolute secrecy, confront an alleged person, conduct an informal investigation, or continue the ordinary support review.

---

## 8. Monthly Coaching Review

At the end of the first month, team leads may review aggregate coaching themes. The review should focus on process improvements, not individual rankings.

| Aggregate question | Result / action |
|---|---|
| Which conversation behavior is consistently strong? | [ ] |
| Which behavior needs a shared refresher? | [ ] |
| Which task, portal, or message template causes repeated confusion? | [ ] |
| Are routine support requests being acknowledged within the approved service level? | [ ] |
| Are confidential concerns being routed through the correct process? | [Handled separately by authorized lead] |
| What one system improvement will be made next month? | [ ] |

Do not publish small-cohort counts, individual scores, distinctive comments, or team-lead rankings. If evidence is limited, label the conclusion **insufficient evidence** rather than inferring a retention or performance problem.

---

## 9. Peer-Coaching Commitment

> “I will coach the process, not judge the person. I will help team leads ask permission, listen without investigating, protect privacy, preserve volunteer choice, recognize confidential boundaries, make realistic commitments, and close the loop safely.”

HMSI’s values guide the practice: **Help Meet** means walking alongside people; **Shine** means helping potential and contribution become visible; **Initiative** means turning that commitment into organized, accountable, and safe action.
