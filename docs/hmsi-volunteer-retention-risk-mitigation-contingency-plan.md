> **Purpose:** Protect the quality, safety, dignity, continuity, and accountability of HMSI’s volunteer-retention implementation while giving team leads a clear response when planned work is delayed, blocked, unsafe, or producing unintended effects.

This plan extends the approved **Volunteer Retention Implementation Timeline and Checklist**. It is designed for operational preparedness, not prediction. Risk ratings are planning judgments and should be reviewed against actual evidence. No retention result, incident frequency, or service outcome should be fabricated to complete this matrix.

---

## 1. Risk Rating Method

Use a simple five-level scale for likelihood and impact. The overall rating should reflect the current residual risk after preventive controls, not only the original concern.

| Score | Likelihood | Impact |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Rare or not expected during the quarter | Minimal disruption; no material safety, privacy, authorization, or service effect |
| 2 | Unlikely but plausible | Limited disruption that can be handled within the team |
| 3 | Possible | Noticeable delay, repeated friction, or moderate service effect |
| 4 | Likely | Significant operational, trust, access, or participation effect |
| 5 | Almost certain or already occurring | Severe safety, privacy, security, authorization, or continuity effect |

**Risk score = likelihood × impact.**

| Score range | Planning level | Required handling |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Monitor through normal owner review. |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Assign a mitigation and review at least monthly. |
| 10–16 | High | Assign an owner, trigger, contingency, and leadership visibility. |
| 17–25 | Critical | Escalate promptly; pause affected work if safety, privacy, security, or authorization is uncertain. |

A high score does not authorize unsafe action. Any safeguarding, privacy, security, fraud, or serious wellbeing concern follows its designated confidential route regardless of the numerical score.

## 3. Detailed Risk Mitigation Matrix

| ID | Risk and consequence | Early-warning signals | Preventive controls | Trigger threshold | Immediate containment | Contingency response | Recovery and verification | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Roles, authority, data definitions, or escalation paths remain unclear, delaying decisions or creating inconsistent action. | Conflicting instructions; unassigned checklist rows; repeated “who approves this?” questions. | Confirm executive sponsor, workstream owners, definitions, access, and confidential routes in Week 1. | Any critical workstream lacks an owner or approval boundary by the scope gate. | Pause the affected decision; record the ambiguity; do not infer authority. | Convene a short decision session with the sponsor and relevant leads; issue a dated decision record and updated responsibility matrix. | Recheck affected materials and test one representative workflow with an authorized account. | [Executive sponsor] |
| R2 | Revised onboarding, role pathways, or first-task materials are not ready, forcing an unclear or inconsistent rollout. | Drafts miss review date; content has contradictory role permissions; no pilot cohort available. | Work backward from the rollout gate; use version control, content review, and a minimum viable safe pathway. | Materials are not approved by the design or pilot gate. | Keep the current approved pathway in place; do not launch unreviewed guidance. | Delay rollout for the affected pathway, provide a short approved interim briefing, and prioritize only essential corrections. | Obtain owner approval, run a safe usability test, and record the new version date before release. | [Volunteer coordinator] |
| R3 | Pilot exposes role, menu, task, room, or authorization confusion that could block participation or expose protected actions. | Pilot users select wrong role; missing or excess menu links; failed task or room boundaries; repeated access errors. | Use role-specific test accounts, least privilege, server-side authorization, and a negative-test checklist. | Any pilot participant can view or mutate another role’s protected data, or cannot reach a required approved route. | Stop the affected flow; preserve logs; remove access if necessary; do not work around the authorization defect. | Roll back or disable the affected change, route work through an approved manual process, and open a corrective task. | Re-test active, cross-role, anonymous, and unauthorized cases before reopening. | [Portal owner] |
| R4 | Portal, task feed, room, email, or support route becomes unavailable, preventing safe participation. | Elevated error responses; failed refresh; repeated timeout; delivery failures; stale status. | Maintain route ownership, smoke checks, documented fallback contact, and a known-good prior process. | Critical route unavailable beyond [duration] or affecting [scope]. | Publish a clear status message; prevent duplicate submissions; preserve already-accepted work. | Use a controlled manual intake or approved alternate channel; extend due dates; avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive data. | Reconcile manual records, confirm no duplicate task or message, restore the normal route, and publish closure. | [Portal / operations lead] |
| R5 | Support volume exceeds capacity, leaving blockers unresolved and reducing trust or task completion. | Growing blocker queue; aged requests; missed response expectation; repeated questions. | Categorize blockers, assign a named owner, publish response expectations, and review queue age weekly. | Any high-impact blocker exceeds [time] or queue age exceeds [threshold]. | Triage safety, access, and deadline-critical issues first; acknowledge remaining requests. | Activate backup support owner, simplify or pause affected tasks, extend deadlines, and provide a temporary FAQ or briefing. | Review queue age, reopened issues, and volunteer support feedback; adjust capacity or process. | [Operations lead] |
| R6 | Volunteers’ availability, location, skills, equipment, or personal circumstances change, reducing participation or creating overload. | More pause requests; declined tasks; missed due dates; signs of overcommitment; uneven workload. | Ask for capacity at assignment; offer task sizes, realistic due dates, flexible participation, and non-punitive pause paths. | A volunteer reports inability to continue safely or a team sees repeated overload. | Remove pressure; pause or reassign the task; protect personal information; confirm a safe next step. | Reduce scope, broadcast only approved suitable work, defer nonessential activity, and support a respectful pause or exit. | Confirm the volunteer’s chosen status; review workload balance and continuation experience without punitive interpretation. | [Volunteer coordinator] |
| R7 | Survey response rate, cohort coverage, or data quality is too weak to support a reliable conclusion. | Missing pathway representation; inconsistent denominators; duplicate or incomplete records; unusually low response. | Send accessible reminders, document the invited population, keep definitions stable, and monitor collection quality. | Any headline measure lacks a defensible denominator or key cohort is absent. | Label the result as insufficient evidence; do not set a definitive target from it. | Extend the response window, use a neutral follow-up, collect a focused question, or run a small qualitative check with consent. | Recalculate with documented exclusions and compare only like-for-like periods. | [Evaluation / coordinator] |
| R8 | Safeguarding, privacy, security, consent, or wellbeing concern is mishandled in ordinary retention work. | Disclosure in a survey comment; unapproved link or file; exposed personal data; consent uncertainty; distress signal. | Train owners, minimize data, use approved routes and storage, protect access, and keep confidential matters separate from dashboards. | Any credible concern about immediate safety, abuse, unauthorized disclosure, compromise, or missing consent. | Stop general analysis of the matter; preserve only necessary evidence; restrict access; use the designated confidential route. | Follow the approved safeguarding, privacy, or security incident process; suspend the affected activity or asset if required. | Authorized lead confirms containment, follow-up, access review, and safe closure; no case details enter general reporting. | [Authorized safeguarding / privacy lead] |
| R9 | Recognition, re-engagement, or retention messaging pressures volunteers to remain active or creates public ranking. | Volunteers hesitate to pause; messages imply obligation; competitive comparisons appear; complaints about pressure. | Use choice-preserving language, truthful recognition, private or consented acknowledgement, and supported pause/exit options. | Any message or practice is reasonably understood as coercive, punitive, or publicly comparative. | Withdraw or correct the message; contact affected volunteers with a neutral clarification. | Replace the campaign with a support-focused check-in; offer pause, reduced participation, or confidential follow-up. | Review message approval, volunteer feedback, and whether participation choices remain voluntary. | [Communications lead] |
| R10 | Too many actions, weak ownership, or insufficient resources cause overdue commitments and implementation fatigue. | Multiple missed dates; owners carry too many actions; no verification evidence; repeated deferrals. | Limit quarter commitments to three to five major improvements; require owner, date, measure, and resource check. | Any high-priority action is overdue by [duration] or lacks capacity/resources. | Reprioritize; stop lower-value work; inform leadership and affected volunteers of changes. | Split the action into a safe minimum and later enhancement; reassign or defer with a written reason and review date. | Close only after verification evidence exists; review workload and planning assumptions. | [Implementation coordinator] |
| R11 | A process, portal, or communication change introduces new errors or worsens the volunteer experience. | Increased support questions after release; higher failed requests; lower clarity signal; repeated regression. | Version changes, pilot safely, preserve prior guidance, monitor after rollout, and define rollback criteria. | Post-release errors exceed [threshold] or a critical workflow fails. | Stop further rollout; preserve affected records; publish a clear correction. | Roll back or disable the change, restore the known-good process, and provide temporary instructions. | Compare before/after signals, re-test the affected route, and document the change decision. | [Change owner] |
| R12 | An external provider, notification route, storage service, or other dependency fails, delaying work or communication. | Provider errors; delivery failures; expired configuration; dependency health warning. | Maintain provider ownership, bounded readiness checks, approved fallback contact, and no hidden dependency in critical workflows. | Dependency failure affects a critical route or remains unresolved beyond [duration]. | Prevent repeated sends or duplicate submissions; record the failure state; avoid exposing provider credentials. | Use an approved in-app notice or manual support route; defer noncritical activity; never claim delivery without provider evidence. | Reconcile events, retry only eligible idempotent operations, and confirm closure from the provider and application records. | [Dependency owner] |

## 4. Contingency Activation Protocol

### Level 0: Monitor

The risk is within tolerance and no trigger has been reached. The owner records the current status and reviews it at the normal cadence.

### Level 1: Team response

A trigger has been reached, but the issue can be contained within the assigned workstream. The owner records the trigger, activates the listed immediate action, and reports status at the next operational review.

### Level 2: Cross-functional response

The issue affects more than one workstream, blocks a milestone, or requires a change in scope, resource, access, or communication. The implementation coordinator convenes the relevant owners, records a decision, and informs the executive sponsor.

### Level 3: Executive or confidential escalation

The issue affects safety, safeguarding, privacy, security, authorization, legal obligations, major continuity, or organizational trust. Pause the affected activity where necessary and route the matter to the authorized executive or confidential lead. Do not continue ordinary retention analysis while a serious concern is unresolved.

## 5. Common Contingency Playbooks

### Playbook A: Rollout delay

Use when onboarding, portal, support, or safety materials are not ready at the rollout gate. Keep the last approved process in place, notify owners and affected volunteers, identify the minimum safe correction, and set a new dated rollout decision. Never launch unreviewed instructions simply to meet the calendar.

### Playbook B: Portal or communication outage

Use when a critical route is unavailable. Publish a short status message, prevent duplicate submissions, use only an approved alternate channel, avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive data, extend deadlines where appropriate, and reconcile all manual records after restoration.

### Playbook C: Capacity reduction

Use when volunteer or team capacity falls. Reassess task suitability, reduce scope, pause nonessential work, reassign only with authorization, and make it easy for volunteers to pause without penalty. Report the capacity change as context rather than as a volunteer failure.

### Playbook D: Insufficient evidence

Use when survey coverage or data quality is too weak for a conclusion. Label the finding as insufficient evidence, avoid definitive claims, extend or improve collection neutrally, and use a focused qualitative check only with appropriate consent and privacy controls.

### Playbook E: Safety, privacy, or security concern

Use when a concern appears in a survey, portal, room, task, file, or communication. Stop ordinary analysis of the matter, limit access, preserve only necessary evidence, and use the designated confidential route. Do not copy raw disclosures into dashboards, emails, or action registers.

### Playbook F: Harmful retention messaging

Use when recognition, re-engagement, or leadership communications create pressure. Withdraw or correct the message, clarify that participation choices remain voluntary, offer pause or reduced-participation support, and review the approval process before sending another message.

### Playbook G: Change regression

Use when a new process performs worse than the approved prior process. Stop further rollout, preserve affected records, restore the known-good route where feasible, communicate the correction, and require a new test before re-release.
