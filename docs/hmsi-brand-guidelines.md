# Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)
## Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0 working standard  
**Prepared for:** Help Meet Shine Initiative  
**Primary use:** Public website, donor communications, stakeholder materials, volunteer communications, social media, programme documents, and administrative templates.

> **Brand promise:** HMSI walks alongside people and communities, helps potential become visible, and organizes practical support for lasting social impact.

---

## 1. Purpose of This Guide

This guide gives HMSI staff, volunteers, partners, designers, writers, and service providers a shared standard for communicating the organization clearly and consistently. It explains what the name means, how the mission should sound, how the visual identity should feel, and how to adapt the brand responsibly across digital and printed materials.

The guide is designed to protect three things at once: **clarity**, so people understand HMSI quickly; **dignity**, so communities are represented respectfully; and **trust**, so donors and stakeholders can recognize responsible, accountable communication.

The standards in this document should be applied with judgment. Programme facts, registration details, impact figures, donor claims, and public announcements must always be checked against the latest approved organizational records before publication.

## 2. Brand Foundations

### 2.1 Organization name

The formal public name is **Help Meet Shine Initiative**. The approved short form is **HMSI**. On first use in a document or communication, write the full name followed by the acronym in parentheses:

> Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)

After the first use, **HMSI** may be used when the audience already understands the reference. Do not replace the organization’s name with unapproved abbreviations, casual nicknames, or decorative spellings.

### 2.2 The meaning of the name

The name is not only a title; it is a compact expression of HMSI’s approach to service.

| Name element | Meaning | Communication implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Help Meet** | Draws from the Biblical framing of a suitable helper, partner, or companion. It signals collaboration, support, upliftment, and walking alongside people rather than acting from above them. | Use partnership language. Say “alongside,” “with,” “listen,” “support,” and “work together.” Avoid language that portrays communities as passive recipients or HMSI as a rescuer above them. |
| **Shine** | Communicates transformation, visibility, potential, and success. It suggests helping individuals and communities move from hardship or obscurity toward dignity, confidence, and recognized value. | Highlight agency, progress, capability, dignity, and possibility. Do not reduce “shine” to a promise of instant transformation or guaranteed outcomes. |
| **Initiative** | Gives the vision formal weight and positions HMSI as an organized nonprofit and social-impact venture rather than an informal gathering. | Communicate structure, responsibility, accountability, partnership, and practical action. Show how ideas become organized service. |

### 2.3 One-sentence explanation

> **HMSI means walking alongside people and communities, helping their potential become visible, and organizing practical support for lasting social impact.**

### 2.4 Short explanation for public channels

> **Help Meet** represents partnership and compassionate support. **Shine** reflects dignity, potential, and transformation. **Initiative** represents our organized commitment to responsible nonprofit action and sustainable community impact.

### 2.5 Extended explanation for the About page and stakeholder materials

> For HMSI, **Help Meet** represents a way of serving with people rather than acting above them. The phrase draws on the Biblical framing of a suitable helper, partner, or companion and reflects collaboration, support, upliftment, and shared responsibility. **Shine** expresses our hope that individuals and communities can move from hardship or obscurity toward dignity, confidence, opportunity, and visible value. **Initiative** gives this purpose an organized structure: a committed nonprofit and social-impact venture that turns compassion into responsible action, partnership, and lasting service.

## 3. Mission, Vision, and Positioning

### 3.1 Mission

> **To strengthen people and communities through practical humanitarian, social-development, and empowerment initiatives delivered responsibly and in partnership with relevant stakeholders.**

The mission should be used in formal organizational descriptions, programme documents, partnership materials, and About-page content. It may be shortened for small spaces, but the ideas of **strengthening**, **practical action**, **responsibility**, and **partnership** should remain visible.

### 3.2 Vision

> **A stronger and more resilient society where vulnerable people and communities have improved opportunities, dignity, and pathways to sustainable wellbeing.**

The vision should guide future-facing communications. It is most appropriate for strategy documents, donor materials, annual reports, presentations, and leadership messages.

### 3.3 Positioning statement

> **HMSI is a Nigerian nonprofit that works alongside vulnerable and underserved communities through humanitarian assistance, education, empowerment, livelihoods, and sustainable community development.**

This statement is a starting point for public descriptions. It should be supported with current, verifiable programme information and should not imply outcomes, geographic reach, partnerships, funding, or credentials that have not been approved for publication.

### 3.4 Brand pillars

| Pillar | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Alongside people** | HMSI listens, collaborates, and respects local knowledge and agency. |
| **Practical support** | Communications connect compassion to concrete programmes, services, skills, resources, or pathways. |
| **Visible potential** | Stories show capability, progress, dignity, and participation rather than only need. |
| **Responsible initiative** | HMSI communicates with transparency, safeguarding awareness, accountability, and respect for evidence. |

## 4. Audience and Desired Response

HMSI communicates with several audiences, each of which needs a slightly different emphasis without losing the central identity.

| Audience | They should understand | Desired response |
|---|---|---|
| Community members and people seeking help | HMSI aims to listen, support, and work responsibly with communities. | Feel respected, informed, and able to identify a safe next step. |
| Donors and supporters | Their support contributes to organized, responsible community action. | Review the cause, understand the use of funds, and support through approved channels. |
| Volunteers and field contributors | Their time, skills, and local knowledge can strengthen community-led work. | Apply through the appropriate route, understand expectations, and participate responsibly. |
| Institutional partners | HMSI has a clear mission, governance posture, and commitment to accountable partnership. | Request due diligence information, explore collaboration, or begin a defined partnership conversation. |
| Media and public audiences | HMSI is a credible source of approved organizational and field information. | Share accurate information and direct audiences to official HMSI sources. |

## 5. Tone of Voice

HMSI should sound **human, grounded, responsible, hopeful, and clear**. The tone is warm without being sentimental, confident without exaggerating, and professional without becoming distant.

### 5.1 Voice principles

| Principle | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Dignified** | Describe people as participants, partners, families, learners, workers, or community members where accurate. | Deficit labels, pity-driven language, stereotypes, or language that removes agency. |
| **Practical** | Explain what is happening, who is responsible, what has been verified, and what the next step is. | Vague promises, inflated impact claims, or unexplained institutional jargon. |
| **Hopeful** | Show possibility, contribution, progress, and pathways to wellbeing. | Guaranteed outcomes, miracle language, or promises that depend on unconfirmed funding. |
| **Accountable** | Distinguish verified facts, reported information, plans, and aspirations. | Presenting estimates, testimonials, or draft information as independently verified fact. |
| **Collaborative** | Use “with,” “alongside,” “in partnership,” and “together” where appropriate. | “We saved,” “we rescued,” or other language that centers HMSI as the sole actor. |
| **Accessible** | Use short sentences, descriptive headings, plain English, and clear calls to action. | Dense paragraphs, unexplained acronyms, or technical language without context. |

### 5.2 Preferred language

Use phrases such as **walk alongside**, **work with communities**, **practical support**, **community-led**, **responsible action**, **dignity and opportunity**, **approved information**, **verified cause**, **clear next step**, and **sustainable wellbeing**.

Use caution with phrases such as **transform lives**, **save communities**, **the poorest**, **voiceless people**, **guaranteed impact**, **urgent donation needed**, and **every donation changes everything**. These may be appropriate only when supported by specific, approved context and used without manipulation.

### 5.3 Calls to action

Calls to action should be specific and honest. Preferred examples include:

- **Learn about HMSI’s work**
- **Support an approved cause**
- **Volunteer your skills**
- **Share verified information**
- **Explore partnership opportunities**
- **Read the full field story**
- **Contact HMSI**

Avoid pressure-based calls such as “Donate now or someone will suffer,” unless a responsible emergency communication has been reviewed and approved.

## 6. Visual Identity

### 6.1 Visual character

HMSI’s visual identity should feel like **grounded humanitarian service with a clear path forward**. It combines a deep, trustworthy base with warm human accents and generous space for photography, stories, and practical calls to action.

The visual system should communicate:

1. **Trust:** deep evergreen and restrained typography create stability.
2. **Warmth:** gold and clay accents add human energy without becoming loud.
3. **Clarity:** cream and white space keep information easy to scan.
4. **Action:** strong headings, clear links, and practical layouts help people know what to do next.

### 6.2 Core colour palette

The following palette reflects the current public-facing HMSI visual direction and should be treated as the working digital standard.

| Colour | Suggested name | Hex | Primary use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Deep Evergreen | HMSI Evergreen | `#17221E` | Hero backgrounds, footer, high-contrast sections, primary dark surfaces |
| Forest Teal | HMSI Teal | `#1E5B49` | Links, secondary headings, actions, supportive emphasis |
| Warm Gold | HMSI Gold | `#E1AD45` | Primary calls to action, highlights, key emphasis, active indicators |
| Warm Parchment | HMSI Parchment | `#F6F4EF` | Page backgrounds, quiet information areas, soft contrast |
| Clay Accent | HMSI Clay | `#B56B3B` | Eyebrows, editorial labels, secondary emphasis, storytelling accents |
| Soft Sage | HMSI Sage | `#E9F0E9` | Positive notices, community-support backgrounds, gentle panels |
| White | HMSI White | `#FFFFFF` | Content surfaces, reversed text areas, breathing space |
| Slate Text | HMSI Slate | `#66716A` | Secondary body text and supporting descriptions |

### 6.3 Colour rules

Use one dominant background and no more than two accent colours in a single content area. Gold should guide attention, not fill every element. Teal should communicate action and trust. Deep evergreen should anchor important sections. Parchment and white should provide rest and readability.

Text must meet accessible contrast requirements. Do not place small gold text on white, slate text on deep evergreen without checking contrast, or light text over an uncontrolled photograph. When in doubt, add a solid or gradient overlay and test the final rendered state.

### 6.4 Typography

HMSI typography should use a highly legible sans-serif system with a strong, compact display style for major headings and a calm, readable style for body text. The existing digital direction uses bold uppercase labels, large editorial headings, and generous line spacing.

| Type role | Treatment | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Display heading | Heavy sans-serif, tight tracking, short line length | Use for page titles and campaign statements. Avoid long all-caps paragraphs. |
| Section heading | Bold sans-serif, sentence case or restrained uppercase | Make the information hierarchy obvious. |
| Eyebrow or label | Small bold uppercase with increased letter spacing | Use for categories such as “Our mission,” “Field stories,” or “Why the name matters.” |
| Body copy | Regular sans-serif with generous line height | Keep paragraphs short and use plain language. |
| Action text | Bold sans-serif with clear verbs | Buttons should describe the destination or action, not only say “Click here.” |

Do not use decorative display fonts, faux handwritten styles, excessive italics, or multiple competing typefaces. Typography should support dignity and clarity rather than create visual noise.

### 6.5 Logo and name lockup

The logo is the primary identifier and should remain visually stable. Use the approved logo asset supplied by HMSI; do not redraw, stretch, rotate, recolour, crop, or add effects to it.

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the logo mark’s central symbol or another consistently defined internal measurement. The logo must remain legible at small sizes. On dark backgrounds, use the approved light or reversed version if available; do not improvise a colour inversion.

When the full name appears beside the logo, use the approved spelling:

> **Help Meet Shine Initiative**

Use the acronym **HMSI** only when the full name has already been established or when the layout requires a compact identifier.

### 6.6 Imagery

HMSI imagery should be documentary, human-centered, and contextual. Show people participating, learning, working, organizing, contributing, or receiving support with dignity. Whenever possible, include the location, programme context, date, and appropriate consent record in the internal asset metadata.

Avoid imagery that humiliates, sensationalizes, exposes private medical or personal information, shows identifiable children without proper permission, or uses hardship as a visual device without context. Do not imply that a person pictured received a specific benefit unless that relationship is confirmed.

Use natural light, authentic moments, and clear focal subjects. Pair images with captions that explain what is known and avoid unsupported claims. Cropping should preserve dignity and should not remove relevant context in a misleading way.

### 6.7 Layout and interface direction

Public pages should feel editorial and spacious rather than crowded. Use strong horizontal sections, clear content bands, restrained rounded elements where they support the existing design, and an obvious path from information to action.

Admin and portal interfaces should prioritize clarity over campaign-style decoration. Keep role-relevant navigation visible, label actions explicitly, and distinguish destructive, review, recovery, and completion actions through text as well as colour.

## 7. Content Design Standards

### 7.1 Headlines

Headlines should state the subject and value clearly. Strong examples include **Support community programmes**, **Why the name matters**, **Read approved field stories**, and **Work with HMSI**. Avoid headlines that make broad claims without evidence, such as **We will change the world** or **The only organization communities can trust**.

### 7.2 Stories and field updates

Every public story should make the status of the information clear. Distinguish between **approved**, **published**, **reported**, **planned**, and **under review**. Attribute community contributors and independent publishers accurately. Preserve editorial review and do not allow a contributor role to imply direct publishing authority if the workflow requires administrator approval.

A story should answer, where appropriate: what happened, where and when, who was involved, what has been verified, what remains uncertain, and what the reader can do next.

### 7.3 Donor and campaign communications

Donation messaging must describe the approved campaign, the supported purpose, the payment route, and any relevant limitations. Do not invent donor testimonials, ratings, supporter counts, or impact figures. Thank-you messages should confirm only verified donation information and should not expose private donor data publicly.

### 7.4 Administrative and operational communications

Portal messages should be self-explanatory. Tell the user what happened, what the status means, what action is available, and what happens next. Destructive actions should use explicit confirmation language. Recovery messages should state the recovery window and indicate when an administrator must intervene.

## 8. Accessibility and Inclusion

Accessibility is part of the HMSI brand, not a separate technical concern.

Use semantic headings in order, visible keyboard focus states, descriptive link text, labelled form fields, meaningful alternative text, and status messages that can be recognized by assistive technology. Do not communicate status by colour alone. Pair colour with a label such as **Published**, **Pending review**, **Completed**, or **Recovery available**.

Maintain readable font sizes, sufficient contrast, responsive layouts, and comfortable touch targets. Avoid flashing effects, autoplay audio, unnecessary motion, and dense blocks of uppercase copy. Respect reduced-motion preferences for animated interfaces.

For public forms and portal flows, explain errors in plain language and identify how the user can correct them. Do not expose account existence, internal role claims, access tokens, reset tokens, or other sensitive implementation details in user-facing errors.

## 9. Channel Applications

### 9.1 Website homepage

The homepage should introduce HMSI quickly, connect the name to the service approach, and provide clear routes to learn, support, volunteer, or partner.

**Recommended homepage introduction:**

> Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI) is a Nigerian nonprofit based in Benin City, Edo State. Our name expresses how we work: we walk alongside people and communities, help their potential become visible, and organize practical support for lasting social impact.

### 9.2 About page

The About page should carry the full explanation of the name beside the organization story, mission, vision, governance, and accountability information. The three-part structure should remain clear:

- **Help Meet:** partnership, support, upliftment, and walking alongside people.
- **Shine:** transformation, visibility, potential, dignity, and recognized value.
- **Initiative:** organized nonprofit action, responsibility, partnership, and sustainable service.

### 9.3 Email newsletter

**Subject:** Introducing Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)

**Opening:**

> We are pleased to introduce Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI), a nonprofit committed to walking alongside individuals and communities through practical humanitarian assistance, education, empowerment, livelihoods, and sustainable development.

**Core explanation:**

> Help Meet represents partnership and compassionate support. Shine reflects transformation, dignity, potential, and helping individuals and communities become visible and empowered. Initiative represents our organized commitment to responsible nonprofit action and lasting social impact.

**Call to action:**

> Learn more about HMSI, support an approved cause, volunteer your skills, or explore a partnership with us.

### 9.4 Social media

Social posts should be shorter, human, and easy to share. Introduce one idea at a time and link to the relevant official page. Avoid using a donation appeal as the only way to explain the organization.

**Example:**

> What does HMSI mean? **Help Meet** is partnership. **Shine** is potential and dignity made visible. **Initiative** is organized, responsible action. Together, Help Meet Shine Initiative means walking alongside people, strengthening communities, and helping potential shine.

### 9.5 Partnership and donor documents

Use the positioning statement, mission, vision, brand pillars, governance information, and approved programme evidence. Keep the tone factual and specific. Explain what HMSI is seeking, what it can contribute, what remains to be confirmed, and how accountability will be maintained.

### 9.6 Portal and admin interfaces

Use plain language, visible role context, and explicit actions. The brand should feel calm and dependable rather than promotional. Portal users should always know whether they are viewing an active task, an archived record, a pending review item, a completed submission, or an administrator-only control.

## 10. Editorial and Brand Governance

All public communications should have a clear owner and an appropriate review level. Routine copy can be reviewed by the responsible communications owner. Sensitive content involving safeguarding, allegations, vulnerable people, personal data, financial claims, emergency incidents, or public institutional commitments requires additional review before publication.

The following checks should be completed before release:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Name | Is the organization name spelled correctly and used consistently? |
| Meaning | Does the message reflect partnership, dignity, potential, and responsible initiative? |
| Facts | Are claims current, sourced, approved, and clearly distinguished from aspiration? |
| People | Are individuals represented with dignity, consent, and appropriate privacy protection? |
| Action | Does the audience know the safe and legitimate next step? |
| Accessibility | Can the content be understood and used with keyboard navigation, assistive technology, and mobile layouts? |
| Security | Does the content avoid secrets, private identifiers, internal authorization details, and unsafe links? |
| Brand | Are logo, colour, typography, imagery, and tone consistent with this guide? |

Brand assets should be stored in the approved shared repository with version names, usage notes, source information, and any consent or licensing records required for the asset.

## 11. Quick Reference

### The HMSI formula

> **Help Meet** — walk alongside people.  
> **Shine** — help potential become visible.  
> **Initiative** — organize responsible action for lasting impact.

### The HMSI writing test

Before publishing, ask:

> Does this message show **who we serve**, **how we work**, **what is known**, and **what the audience can do next**?

### The HMSI visual test

Before approving a design, ask:

> Does this look **trustworthy, warm, clear, human, and ready for practical action**?

### The HMSI dignity test

Before publishing an image or story, ask:

> Does this communication protect the person’s dignity, agency, privacy, and right to be represented accurately?
