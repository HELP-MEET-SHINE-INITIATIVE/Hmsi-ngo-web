# HMSI First-Month Volunteer Risk Pulse Check
## Feedback Mechanism for Monitoring Implementation Triggers

**Organization:** Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)  
**Use period:** First month of the retention implementation quarter  
**Audience:** New volunteers, volunteer coordinators, team leads, operations leads, portal owners, and authorized safeguarding/privacy/security leads  
**Recommended completion time:** 3–5 minutes  
**Cadence:** Day 3–5, Day 14, and Day 28 after onboarding; optional event-based check after a first task, support blocker, or reported concern

> **Purpose:** Detect early friction, support needs, and trust risks while there is still time to help. This pulse check is not a performance score, attendance tracker, or requirement to remain active.

---

## 1. Operating Model

The pulse check combines three lightweight signals:

| Signal | Source | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Volunteer pulse** | Short self-report form | Understand clarity, capacity, connection, confidence, and safety from the volunteer’s perspective. |
| **Operational signal** | Approved task, support, and portal records | Identify blockers such as failed access, unclear assignments, unresolved requests, or repeated proof-submission problems. |
| **Owner review** | Weekly team-lead review | Confirm whether a trigger needs support, correction, monitoring, or confidential referral. |

No single signal proves that a volunteer will disengage or that a team has failed. The signals are used to start a proportionate support conversation or operational correction.

The mechanism reflects HMSI’s values. **Help Meet** means responding alongside the volunteer. **Shine** means making contribution and support needs visible without exposing people. **Initiative** means turning early signals into organized, accountable action.

---

## 2. Pulse Schedule

| Checkpoint | Timing | Main purpose | Default owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pulse 1: Orientation** | Day 3–5 | Confirm welcome, role clarity, access, support contact, and safety basics. | Volunteer coordinator |
| **Pulse 2: First contribution** | Day 14 or after first task | Confirm task fit, confidence, support response, proof workflow, and workload. | Team lead or task owner |
| **Pulse 3: Continuation** | Day 28 | Confirm sustainable participation, next step, inclusion, and unresolved concerns. | Volunteer coordinator |
| **Event-based pulse** | Within 1–2 working days of a significant blocker or concern | Offer support after a failed workflow, missed handoff, or reported uncertainty. | Relevant owner |

A reminder should be neutral and optional. Volunteers may answer anonymously where practical, skip a question, request follow-up, or choose to pause participation. Do not send repeated reminders that imply obligation.

## 4. Trigger and Response Matrix

Pulse responses should be combined with operational evidence and context. The thresholds below are starting controls and should be adjusted after the first month of use.

| Trigger | Suggested threshold | Classification | First response | Escalation or contingency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity trigger** | Score 1–2 on Question 1, or repeated role-boundary confusion | Support / delivery | Send a plain-language explanation and name the authorized contact. | Review role materials; pause the task if authorization is uncertain. |
| **Capacity trigger** | Score 1–2 on Question 2, a pause request, or repeated missed/declined tasks | People / capacity | Ask what level of participation is sustainable; reduce or reassign work with consent. | Activate pause or respectful-exit path; avoid punitive interpretation. |
| **Connection trigger** | Score 1–2 on Question 3, an unanswered support request, or blocker beyond [time] | Operations | Acknowledge the request and assign an owner. | Activate backup support; extend deadlines; review queue capacity. |
| **Confidence trigger** | Score 1–2 on Question 4, failed first-task flow, or repeated proof errors | Task / portal | Provide a worked example, check-in, or supervised next step. | Roll back unclear guidance or pause the affected workflow until corrected. |
| **Safety and trust trigger** | Score 1–2 on Question 5, or any report of feeling unsafe, pressured, excluded, or uncertain about confidentiality | Safety / trust | Stop ordinary analysis; offer confidential contact and protect access to the response. | Use the designated safeguarding, privacy, security, or wellbeing route. |
| **System availability trigger** | Required portal, room, support, or notification route unavailable beyond [duration] | Technology / continuity | Publish a bounded status update and prevent duplicate submissions. | Use the approved manual fallback; reconcile records after recovery. |
| **Evidence-quality trigger** | Response count or cohort coverage too weak for a conclusion | Evidence | Label the result insufficient evidence; do not set a target from it. | Extend collection neutrally or use an approved qualitative check. |
| **Messaging-pressure trigger** | Volunteer says recognition or follow-up feels coercive, competitive, or punitive | Culture / communications | Correct or withdraw the message and clarify choice. | Review communications approval and offer pause/reduced participation. |

A single low score should normally create a support opportunity, not an automatic escalation. Any credible safety, safeguarding, privacy, or security concern is handled through the appropriate confidential route regardless of score.

## 5. Owner Response Service Levels

These are operating targets for response, not promises to disclose case details or bypass confidential processes.

| Signal | Acknowledge | Initial action | Review |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Routine clarity or task question | Within [1 working day] | Provide guidance or assign an owner | Within [3 working days] |
| Portal or assignment blocker | Within [1 working day] | Triage impact and protect due date | Within [2 working days] |
| Capacity or pause request | Within [1 working day] | Confirm choice and adjust expectations | Within [3 working days] |
| Reported exclusion, pressure, or wellbeing concern | Promptly and privately | Offer safe contact and route appropriately | Authorized lead determines |
| Safety, safeguarding, privacy, or security concern | According to approved incident route | Stop ordinary handling and escalate confidentially | Authorized lead determines |
| Service outage affecting participation | Status update within [time] | Activate continuity route | At recovery and after-action review |

Do not publish these service levels unless HMSI has the capacity to meet them. If a response will be delayed, acknowledge the request and provide a safe next update.

## 7. Escalation Decision Tree

**Step 1 — Is there an immediate safety, safeguarding, privacy, security, harassment, or serious wellbeing concern?**  
If yes, stop ordinary analysis, restrict access to the minimum necessary, preserve only appropriate evidence, and use the designated confidential route. Do not wait for the weekly meeting.

**Step 2 — Is the volunteer blocked from safe or authorized participation?**  
If yes, pause or protect the affected task, assign an owner, provide a safe interim instruction, and extend the deadline where appropriate.

**Step 3 — Is the issue a routine clarity, capacity, connection, or confidence problem?**  
If yes, respond through the owner service level, record the minimum operational information, and check whether the next pulse improves.

**Step 4 — Is the evidence too weak or mixed for a conclusion?**  
If yes, label it as monitoring or insufficient evidence, avoid broad claims, and improve collection or follow-up.

**Step 5 — Did the response resolve the trigger?**  
If yes, record the verification measure and close or monitor. If no, activate the relevant contingency playbook and raise the issue at the next decision gate.

## 8. Contingency Actions by Roadblock

| Roadblock | Fallback action during the first month | Recovery condition |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding materials delayed | Use the last approved guidance plus a short authorized briefing; defer nonessential rollout. | New materials approved and safely tested. |
| Portal route unavailable | Use approved manual support or intake; prevent duplicate submissions and protect sensitive data. | Normal route restored and manual records reconciled. |
| Support backlog grows | Triage high-impact blockers, activate backup owner, extend deadlines, and publish a clear next update. | Queue age returns within the agreed service level. |
| Volunteer capacity drops | Reduce scope, reassign with consent, or support a pause. | Volunteer confirms a sustainable next step. |
| Low response coverage | Extend the response window neutrally and label findings insufficient evidence. | Defensible response count and documented denominator. |
| Low confidence or repeated proof errors | Provide a worked example and supervised task; pause unclear workflow. | Volunteer completes the corrected flow or chooses another suitable task. |
| Safety or privacy concern | Use confidential escalation; remove the matter from general dashboard reporting. | Authorized lead confirms safe handling and closure. |
| Harmful retention message | Withdraw or correct the message and clarify that participation remains voluntary. | Communication review completed and affected volunteers supported. |

## 9. Privacy and Data-Minimization Controls

The pulse check should collect only information needed to improve support or route a concern. Names, contact details, role, pathway, and participation mode should be optional unless needed for an authorized follow-up. Store raw responses separately from aggregate dashboard summaries and limit access to approved reviewers.

Do not use pulse responses to infer motivation, personal circumstances, competence, health, or future participation. Do not expose small cohorts, distinctive comments, or individual scores in leadership materials. Do not combine confidential safety matters with a general retention score.

Use anonymized paraphrases for ordinary learning. For safety, safeguarding, privacy, security, or wellbeing concerns, record only a route/status signal in the general review and maintain case details in the authorized confidential process.

## 10. First-Month Implementation Checklist

### Before launch

- [ ] Confirm the form owner, response channel, storage location, and access permissions.
- [ ] Confirm the designated confidential safeguarding, privacy, security, and wellbeing routes.
- [ ] Confirm pulse timing and neutral reminder language.
- [ ] Configure the five questions, issue categories, pause options, and follow-up consent.
- [ ] Define the response owner and service level for each trigger.
- [ ] Test the form with non-sensitive fixtures and confirm that no secret or private case data is requested.

### During Week 1

- [ ] Send Pulse 1 after orientation.
- [ ] Review clarity, access, connection, and safety signals.
- [ ] Resolve or route critical blockers before assigning additional work.
- [ ] Record aggregate counts and anonymized themes only.

### During Weeks 2–3

- [ ] Send Pulse 2 after first contribution or at Day 14.
- [ ] Review task fit, confidence, proof guidance, and support response.
- [ ] Check for capacity or pause requests without punitive interpretation.
- [ ] Test whether any corrective action reduced the original trigger.

### During Week 4

- [ ] Send Pulse 3 at Day 28.
- [ ] Review continuation preferences and sustainable next steps.
- [ ] Close, monitor, or escalate open triggers.
- [ ] Prepare a privacy-preserving “what we heard and what we are changing” update.
- [ ] Record recommendations for the next cohort and quarter.

## 12. Success Criteria for the First Month

The mechanism is working when volunteers can complete the pulse check without confusion or pressure, team leads can identify and own routine blockers, critical concerns are routed confidentially, and the organization can show what changed in response to feedback. The goal is not a perfect score or a high response rate at any cost.

At the end of the month, assess whether:

| Criterion | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Volunteers understand the purpose and voluntary nature of the pulse | Response experience or support feedback | [Result] |
| Trigger definitions are consistent | Completed review logs | [Result] |
| Routine issues receive timely ownership | Blocker and response records | [Result] |
| Critical matters stay out of general reporting | Governance review | [Result] |
| Pause and reduced-participation choices remain respected | Support/continuation records | [Result] |
| Corrective actions are verified rather than assumed | Before/after or follow-up evidence | [Result] |
| Next-month changes are prioritized | Approved action register | [Result] |

> **HMSI standard:** Listen early, respond proportionately, protect people and information, and make the next safe step clear.
