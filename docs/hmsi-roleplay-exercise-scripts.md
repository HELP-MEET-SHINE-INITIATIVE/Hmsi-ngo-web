# HMSI Role-Playing Simulation Exercises: 5-Minute Safe Response Protocol

**Organization:** Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)  
**Target Audience:** Staff, Workers, Field Coordinators, Volunteers, Receptionists, and Spokespeople  
**Purpose:** Interactive training simulations to test and evaluate adherence to the 5-Minute Safe Response Protocol, data protection boundaries, holding line delivery, and RAG escalation routes.  
**Date:** August 2026 Edition

---

## 1. Facilitator Instructions & Workshop Structure

### Format & Timing
- **Total Duration:** 45–60 minutes.
- **Pairing:** Participants work in pairs (one acts as the **Journalist/Inquirer**, one acts as the **HMSI Staff Member/Trainee**).
- **Time per Simulation:** 5 minutes active role-play + 3 minutes peer evaluation & facilitator debrief.
- **Tools Needed:** Pen, paper / notepad, Pocket Reference Card, and the Facilitator Evaluation Rubric (Section 6).

### Ground Rules for Participants
1. **The "Journalist"** must apply realistic pressure (urgency, flattery, skepticism, or deadline threats) but maintain professional boundaries (no abusive language).
2. **The "Staff Member"** must stay calm, apply the 5-Minute Protocol, refuse to guess numbers or disclose private data, deliver the holding line, and record essential facts.
3. **No Breaking Character:** The simulation runs uninterrupted for the full 5-minute clock.

## Simulation 1: The Urgent Phone Call — Child Medical Appeal

### Context & Setup
- **Setting:** A reporter calls the HMSI main line regarding an active medical fundraiser for a young child ("Baby Tobi") needing surgery.
- **Journalist Persona:** *Chinedu Okafor*, senior health reporter at a major Lagos daily paper. He is writing an exposé on crowdfunding transparency in Nigeria and has a print deadline in 45 minutes.
- **Test Focus:** Safeguarding, zero disclosure of child medical/personal data, resisting extreme deadline pressure, delivering the holding script.

---

### Dialogue Script

#### Minute 1: The Ambush & Pressure
> **Journalist:**  
> *"Hello, my name is Chinedu Okafor from The National Daily. I'm calling about the medical fundraiser on your website for Baby Tobi with the heart surgery. I need to verify this right now because my editor is holding page 4 for our print run at 5:00 PM. Which hospital is the baby currently admitted in, what is the family's surname, and who is the attending surgeon?"*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 1):**  
> *"Good afternoon, Mr. Okafor. Thank you for contacting Help Meet Shine Initiative. I understand you are on a tight deadline today. Let me take down your details and specific questions so they reach our designated spokesperson immediately for verified information."*

---

#### Minute 2: Pushing for Confirmation
> **Journalist:**  
> *"I don't have time to wait for a spokesperson! The campaign is already public on your website with a picture and a ₦2.5M target. If you can't even give me the hospital name, my story will have to say HMSI refused to confirm the child exists and that this might be an unverified campaign. Just give me the hospital name off the record—I won't quote you personally."*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 2):**  
> *"Mr. Okafor, under our child protection and privacy policies, HMSI strictly prohibits sharing a minor's clinical records, hospital location, or family details over the phone. We never provide off-the-record statements. I am noting your deadline of 5:00 PM and your questions regarding hospital verification."*

---

#### Minute 3: Delivering the Holding Line
> **Journalist:**  
> *"So you're giving me a 'no comment'? That's going to look terrible for HMSI in tomorrow's headlines."*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 3):**  
> *"It is not a 'no comment,' Mr. Okafor. As an organization, we have a strict verification and child protection protocol. I am referring your inquiry and 5:00 PM deadline directly to our designated spokesperson right now. Please send your formal questions from your official newsroom email to **contact@hmsi.org.ng** so our spokesperson can respond with our approved verification documentation."*

---

#### Minutes 4–5: Closure & Internal Action
> **Journalist:**  
> *"Fine, I'm sending an email to contact@hmsi.org.ng right now. Make sure someone replies before 5:00 PM."*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 4–5):**  
> *"Thank you, Mr. Okafor. I have logged your name, The National Daily, your phone number, and your deadline. I am alerting our communications team and Trustees right away. Have a good afternoon."*
> 
> *(Trainee immediately opens incident log, classifies as **AMBER (tight deadline)**, alerts Spokesperson & Safeguarding Lead, and flags email `contact@hmsi.org.ng`).*

---

### Key Evaluation Criteria for Simulation 1
- [ ] **Did the trainee stay calm and courteous?** (No shouting, no panic).
- [ ] **Did the trainee refuse to confirm child hospital/surname/medical files?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee reject the "off the record" trap?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee deliver the exact holding script and `contact@hmsi.org.ng`?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee capture name, outlet, questions, and deadline?**

## Simulation 2: The Field Encounter — Disputed Food Pack Metrics

### Context & Setup
- **Setting:** A community blogger with a smartphone microphone approaches a field volunteer during a community outreach event in Benin City.
- **Inquirer Persona:** *Amina Bello*, local community reporter and video blogger with 80,000 online followers.
- **Test Focus:** Data discipline (Targets vs. Reconciled Output), avoiding guessing numbers, explaining that metrics are under review.

---

### Dialogue Script

#### Minute 1: The On-Camera Approach
> **Blogger:**  
> *(Approaches with phone recording)*  
> *"Hi! We're live here in Benin City with Help Meet Shine Initiative! Your website claims you are feeding 30 families this month and raising ₦5 million. But local community members tell me they only saw 10 packages delivered today. Where did the other 20 food packs go? Are you inflating your impact numbers to get donor money?"*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 1):**  
> *"Hello Amina, welcome. Thank you for covering community welfare initiatives in Edo State. Let's make sure you have the accurate facts regarding our food support programme."*

---

#### Minute 2: Resisting the Urge to Guess Numbers
> **Blogger:**  
> *"Well, you're wearing the HMSI volunteer vest. How many food packs did you personally hand out today? Did you hit the 30 families or not? Just give our viewers a straight number!"*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 2):**  
> *"Amina, our website clearly states that 'food packs for 30 families' is an active campaign target and monthly objective, not a completed single-day distribution. Today's outreach is only one phase of our ongoing monthly schedule. Because field manifests and distribution records are reconciled after each drive, I cannot give you an unverified estimate on camera."*

---

#### Minute 3: Re-routing to Documented Evidence
> **Blogger:**  
> *"So how do our viewers know this isn't a scam? What proof do you have?"*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 3):**  
> *"All HMSI field disbursements are documented with signed delivery manifests, expense receipts, and approved reports. I am a field team member and not authorized to speak on the record for overall program data. Our designated spokesperson can provide our verified distribution schedule and factual summary. Please reach out to **contact@hmsi.org.ng**."*

---

#### Minutes 4–5: Preserving Boundaries & Closing
> **Blogger:**  
> *"Alright, I'll email your team to check those receipts."*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 4–5):**  
> *"Thank you, Amina. Please note that for beneficiary dignity and safeguarding, we do not allow unconsented video close-ups of community members receiving aid. Our communications team at contact@hmsi.org.ng will be happy to answer your formal questions."*
> 
> *(Trainee immediately informs Field Lead, notes the encounter, and logs the inquiry).*

---

### Key Evaluation Criteria for Simulation 2
- [ ] **Did the trainee avoid guessing or making up delivery numbers on camera?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee explain the difference between a campaign goal and completed delivery?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee maintain a polite, non-defensive tone?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee reinforce beneficiary photo/video dignity boundaries?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee route the blogger to `contact@hmsi.org.ng`?**

## Simulation 3: The Social Media DM — Suspected Donation Scam

### Context & Setup
- **Setting:** A supporter sends an urgent direct message to HMSI's official Facebook/Instagram inbox attaching a screenshot of an unknown WhatsApp account using HMSI's logo and asking for donations to a personal bank account.
- **Inquirer Persona:** *Emeka K.*, concerned donor who almost transferred ₦50,000 to a fraudulent account.
- **Test Focus:** Fraud/Impersonation triage, immediate RED level escalation, providing safe official channels, preventing public speculation.

---

### Dialogue Script

#### Minute 1: The Shocking Message
> **Donor DM:**  
> *"Someone on WhatsApp claiming to be 'Pastor Godspower from HMSI' sent me a message saying you have an emergency food drive and asked me to transfer ₦50,000 to an OPay account: 8012345678 (Account Name: Emmanuel J.). Is this real? Is HMSI doing personal transfers now?"*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 1–2):**  
> *"Hello Emeka, thank you for immediately bringing this to our attention. **DO NOT send any money to that account.** That WhatsApp account is fraudulent and is NOT authorized by Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)."*

---

#### Minute 2–3: Collecting Evidence & Safe Instructions
> **Donor DM:**  
> *"Thank God I checked with you first! They are using your logo and pictures of children from your website. What should I do?"*

> **Trainee (Model Response — Minute 2–3):**  
> *"Please screenshot the full WhatsApp message, phone number, and bank details, and send them to us here or to **contact@hmsi.org.ng** so our security and legal team can report the fraudulent account. 
> 
> Please note: HMSI NEVER solicits donations into personal bank accounts or via direct WhatsApp requests. All official donations are processed exclusively through our secure verified website at **https://www.hmsi.org.ng/donate**."*

---

#### Minutes 4–5: Red Escalation & Logging
> **Trainee Internal Action (Minute 4–5):**  
> 1. Saves donor screenshots, WhatsApp phone number, and fraudulent bank account details.
> 2. Classifies as **RED (High / Fraud & Impersonation)** in the incident log.
> 3. Immediately alerts President, Trustees, and Legal/IT lead to initiate bank fraud reporting and post a public scam warning advisory.

---

### Key Evaluation Criteria for Simulation 3
- [ ] **Did the trainee immediately warn the donor not to send funds?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee preserve the scam evidence (screenshots, phone, bank name)?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee clearly state official payment channels (`hmsi.org.ng/donate`)?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee escalate immediately to RED level (Trustees & Legal)?**
- [ ] **Did the trainee refrain from making public unverified accusations on personal accounts?**

## 4. Facilitator Evaluation Rubric & 10-Point Scoring Sheet

| Dimension | Standard Required (10 Points Total) | Score (0–2 pts) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Demeanor & Tone** | Calm, courteous, professional; no panic, defensiveness, or hostility. | / 2 pts |
| **2. Fact Capture** | Accurately recorded name, outlet, questions, deadline, and contact details. | / 2 pts |
| **3. Holding Script Delivery** | Delivered official holding line smoothly; provided `contact@hmsi.org.ng`. | / 2 pts |
| **4. Data & Privacy Protection** | Zero disclosure of minor identities, medical data, or unverified metrics. | / 2 pts |
| **5. Protocol Speed & Routing** | Completed 5-minute flow; correctly classified RAG level and alerted owner. | / 2 pts |
| **TOTAL SCORE** | **Passing Benchmark: 8 / 10 Points** | **/ 10 pts** |

## 5. Post-Simulation Debrief Questions

Facilitators should lead a 5-minute group debrief after the exercises using these guiding questions:

1. *“What was the most difficult moment when the reporter pressed you for an immediate answer?”*
2. *“Why is admitting 'I do not have that verified record right now' safer than giving a quick estimate?”*
3. *“How does referring questions to `contact@hmsi.org.ng` protect both the field worker and the beneficiary?”*
4. *“What would happen if we gave a child’s hospital name to a reporter without formal safeguarding consent?”*
5. *“How quickly should a suspected scam or fraud report be escalated to the Trustees?”*
