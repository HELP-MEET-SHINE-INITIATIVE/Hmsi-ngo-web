# HMSI Website and Press-Release Neutrality Review

**Review date:** 22 August 2026

## Scope and conclusion

This review examined HMSI’s global metadata, homepage, About page, transparency page, partnerships page, volunteer metadata, impact page, homepage support CTA, fundraising-growth component, newsroom interface, public news listing, and public article metadata.

The objective was not to turn a fundraising website into an encyclopedia. Ordinary donation, volunteer, support, and partnership calls to action are legitimate on HMSI’s own website. The objective was to ensure that public descriptions do not present self-reported claims as independent verification, use unsupported superlatives as facts, or blur HMSI’s own editorial content with external journalism.

The public news endpoint returned HTTP 200 with `articles: []` during the review. No currently published HMSI newsroom articles or press releases were available for article-level wording correction. The newsroom interface was updated so future submissions are described as HMSI-published updates and factual field updates, not as independently verified news.

## Neutrality principles applied

Wikipedia’s neutral-point-of-view and conflict-of-interest guidance applies to Wikipedia articles, not directly to ordinary NGO fundraising copy. However, the same evidence discipline improves HMSI’s public credibility. The review applied the following rules:

| Risk | Correction rule |
|---|---|
| Promotional slogan presented as description | Replace with a descriptive heading or programme label |
| Self-reported result presented as established fact | Use “HMSI-reported,” state the reporting period and methodology requirement, or remove the claim |
| “Verified” used without explaining who verified it | Use “approved,” “published,” or “subject to HMSI review” where that is what the process actually establishes |
| Testimonial or maxim presented without source | Label it as HMSI programme information or remove it from encyclopedia-oriented copy |
| HMSI newsroom content presented as independent journalism | Identify it as published HMSI content; reserve “independent coverage” for unrelated editorial sources |
| Ranking described as impact | Describe the data operation, such as “highest recorded fundraising totals” |

## Corrections applied

| Area | Previous risk | Current treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Global metadata | Donation-oriented promotional descriptions and “support communities” positioning | Factual organization and programme descriptions |
| About metadata and hero | “Restore hope,” “empowering communities,” “beacon of hope,” and slogan-style headline | Describes HMSI’s stated activities and identifies the organization by name |
| About metrics | “7 meals,” “100+,” and award language could be read as independently established | Labels figures as HMSI-reported and states that reporting period, methodology, or independent evidence require confirmation |
| Homepage hero | “Help communities thrive” and “lasting community change” | “Support community programmes” and a factual description of the organization and available actions |
| Homepage impact section | “Numbers with names behind them,” life-growth language, and unqualified totals | “Reported programme indicators” and an explicit HMSI-reporting qualifier |
| Homepage stories and testimonial-style module | Emotional headlines and unattributed maxim | “Field stories and updates” and “Published HMSI programme information” |
| Fundraising ranking | “Top impact fundraising” and “causes moving furthest” | Approved fundraisers ordered by recorded progress, with a prompt to review each campaign page |
| Homepage newsletter | “Success stories,” “impact we are making,” and “No spam. Just hope.” | Published field updates, participation information, and unsubscribe wording |
| Help CTA | “Get the help you need with dignity,” “HMSI is here for you,” and implied support outcome | “Submit a support request,” explains review before public fundraising, and points to privacy information |
| Fundraising-growth hub | “Turn community energy into practical support,” “lead accountable growth,” and ambassador-style promotional labels | Describes campaign participation pathways and approved-information requirements |
| Transparency page | “Built to serve responsibly,” “high standards,” “make verification easier,” and “trust is our foundation” | Governance, accountability, due-diligence, and evidence-status headings; self-descriptions are attributed to HMSI where appropriate |
| Partnerships page | “Together we are one,” “move from crisis to possibility,” “partner-ready foundation,” and “join the fight” | Factual partnership information, programme options, and due-diligence language |
| Newsroom interface and metadata | “Verified news” and metadata describing an update as verified | “Factual field updates,” “published HMSI update,” and “published humanitarian or community update” |
| Footer | Brand slogan and “move from crisis to possibility” | Factual organizational description and public contact wording |

## Language that remains intentionally persuasive

Buttons such as “Donate,” “Volunteer,” “Request support,” and “Contact HMSI” remain because they are functional calls to action rather than claims of fact. Terms such as “approved fundraiser” may remain where HMSI’s internal workflow actually approves a campaign; they should not be described as independently verified unless an independent verifier is identified.

The organization’s mission, vision, values, safeguarding commitments, and programme descriptions are naturally written from HMSI’s perspective. When reused in a future Wikipedia draft, those statements should be attributed to HMSI or replaced with independent sources. They do not establish notability by themselves.

## Press-release controls for future publications

Before publishing a newsroom item, HMSI should record the event date and location, source of each factual figure, author, image consent status, safeguarding review, and whether any partner or sponsor supplied language. A published HMSI update should not be described as independent media coverage. If an unrelated outlet later reports on the work, preserve the original URL, author, date, relevant passages, and evidence that the piece was not copied from an HMSI release.

Future releases should avoid “best,” “leading,” “award-winning,” “life-changing,” “transformative,” “high-impact,” “trusted,” and similar terms unless they are directly quoted and attributed to a named source. Even then, the wording should not imply that the quoted assessment is an objective HMSI fact.

## Remaining review items

The following are not automatically violations, but they require evidence or careful attribution before appearing in any Wikipedia article:

- HMSI-reported meal-efficiency figures.
- HMSI-reported field-team numbers.
- HMSI-reported people-supported, project, state-reach, income, or programme-share figures.
- The reported 2022 MEA award.
- Any testimonial, beneficiary quote, or field-story claim.
- Claims that a request is “verified” or that funds are “securely” disbursed directly to a service provider.
- Statements about the geographic scale of activity across Africa.

The safest future Wikipedia workflow remains disclosure of HMSI’s connection to the draft, use of Articles for Creation, and citation of independent secondary publications rather than HMSI’s own website or newsroom.
