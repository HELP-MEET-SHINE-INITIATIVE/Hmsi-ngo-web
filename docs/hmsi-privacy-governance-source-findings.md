# HMSI Privacy Governance Source Findings

## Authoritative sources reviewed

1. Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC), Resources: https://ndpc.gov.ng/resources/
   - The regulator publishes the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 and implementation resources.
   - The official site provides privacy-related services including data-controller/processor registration and privacy-breach reporting.

2. NDPC, Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025: https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf
   - Official 117-page directive reviewed from the regulator-hosted PDF.
   - The directive is the primary operational reference for translating the NDPA into organizational controls.

## Drafting anchors

The accompanying HMSI policy should be written as an internal governance standard, not as a legal opinion. It should require legal/DPO validation of HMSI's actual controller/processor status, registration obligations, cross-border transfers, contracts, and breach-notification decisions.

The policy should operationalize privacy by design, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, confidentiality, least-privilege access, documented retention, processor oversight, data-subject request handling, incident response, and auditable accountability. For volunteer feedback and retention analytics, the default output should be aggregate and cohort-suppressed: patterns, not people.

Safeguarding reports, health or disability information, allegations, identity documents, and other high-risk material must be segregated from ordinary retention analytics and routed through confidential safeguarding or incident channels.

No specific statutory deadline, registration category, or legal basis should be asserted as universally applicable without confirming HMSI's current facts and the current NDPC instrument. The final document should cite the NDPA/GAID as the governing references and label implementation values such as retention periods, response targets, and suppression thresholds as HMSI policy choices subject to review.
