# Welcome to Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)
## New Team Member and Volunteer Onboarding Guide

**Version:** 1.0 working guide  
**Audience:** New staff, workers, volunteers, members, community publishers, humanitarian activists, and independent field reporters  
**Core message:** Help Meet. Shine. Initiative.

> Welcome to HMSI. You are joining a community committed to walking alongside people, strengthening communities, and helping potential shine through responsible action.

---

## 1. Welcome

Thank you for choosing to contribute to the work of **Help Meet Shine Initiative (HMSI)**. Whether you are joining as a team member, worker, volunteer, member, community publisher, humanitarian activist, or independent field reporter, your role begins with the same commitment: to serve people with dignity, work responsibly with others, and help turn good intentions into practical, accountable action.

HMSI is a Nigerian nonprofit working through humanitarian assistance, education, empowerment, livelihoods, and sustainable community development. We believe meaningful service is done **with** people and communities, not simply **for** them or above them.

This guide explains what HMSI stands for, how we expect people to work, how role-based participation operates, and what to do during your first days. Keep it available as a reference and ask for clarification whenever a task, instruction, safety concern, or communication is unclear.

---

## 2. What the HMSI Name Means

The HMSI name is a practical guide to our behaviour.

| Name element | What it means for your work |
|---|---|
| **Help Meet** | We walk alongside people as partners, helpers, and companions. Listen first, respect local knowledge, and support agency rather than presenting yourself as a rescuer. |
| **Shine** | We help potential, dignity, progress, and contribution become visible. Share stories responsibly and recognize people for their strengths, not only their needs. |
| **Initiative** | We organize compassion into responsible action. Follow agreed processes, document work accurately, protect information, and complete commitments reliably. |

When deciding how to act, ask three questions: **Am I working alongside people? Am I helping dignity and potential become visible? Am I acting through a responsible and organized process?**

## 4. Your Role and Participation Pathway

HMSI uses role-based access so each person sees the tools and tasks relevant to their responsibilities. Your role does not make you superior or inferior to another participant; it defines your authorized responsibilities and the information you need to perform them safely.

| Pathway | Typical contribution | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Worker** | Field operations, logistics, coordination, physical intervention, and assigned operational duties. | Complete only authorized tasks, protect sensitive field information, and submit proof through the approved task workflow. |
| **Volunteer** | Community outreach, local verification, ground assistance, community check-ins, or approved publisher pathways. | Volunteers with publisher access may submit material for editorial review but cannot publish directly to the public feed. |
| **Member** | Advocacy, amplification, learning, public engagement, and approved member activities. | Share only approved information and do not represent yourself as an HMSI spokesperson unless authorized. |
| **Community Publisher** | Draft community updates and field dispatches for editorial consideration. | All submissions remain subject to HMSI editorial review and approval. |
| **Humanitarian Activist** | Share responsible advocacy and community-focused field information. | Protect the safety, privacy, and dignity of people represented in every submission. |
| **Independent Field Reporter** | Submit accurate field observations, images, and context for review. | Clearly separate personal observation from verified HMSI organizational statements. |
| **HMSI Staff or Administrator** | Coordinate programmes, review submissions, assign work, manage records, and approve public actions according to authority. | Use elevated access only for legitimate organizational purposes and keep an auditable record of consequential actions. |

Your role may determine your portal dashboard, menu items, rooms, task feed, and approval rights. If you see information or an action that does not match your role, do not attempt to work around the control. Report it to an authorized administrator.

## 5. How We Work Together

HMSI work should be clear, respectful, and traceable. When you receive an assignment, confirm that you understand the objective, expected outcome, priority, due date, location or coverage area, safety considerations, and required proof. If any of these are missing, ask before beginning.

Use the official portal and approved communication channels for work-related updates. Keep personal opinions separate from official HMSI statements. Do not promise funds, services, employment, emergency assistance, programme placement, or partnership outcomes unless the promise has been authorized and can be fulfilled.

If you cannot complete a task, update its status promptly and explain the blocker. A timely, honest update is more valuable than an inaccurate completion claim. When a task requires a Google Drive link or other proof, submit the correct link through the assigned workflow and confirm that access permissions support the authorized reviewer.

## 6. Safeguarding, Privacy, and Ethical Conduct

HMSI participants must help maintain a safe environment for community members, colleagues, partners, and volunteers. Safeguarding concerns include abuse, exploitation, harassment, intimidation, discrimination, coercion, unsafe conduct, misuse of authority, and inappropriate contact with vulnerable people.

Do not investigate allegations independently, confront a suspected perpetrator, promise secrecy, or share sensitive details in public rooms. Record only the necessary facts and report the concern through the designated HMSI safeguarding or administrative route as soon as it is safe to do so. If there is immediate danger, prioritize emergency services and personal safety according to the applicable local context.

Protect personal information. Do not copy passwords, access tokens, reset links, identification documents, donor details, beneficiary registers, private phone numbers, or sensitive case information into public chats, screenshots, social media, or unapproved storage. Use the minimum information needed for the task.

Images, interviews, and stories require appropriate consent and context. Do not identify a person, child, survivor, patient, or vulnerable household unless there is a legitimate approved purpose and the required permission. Never use a person’s hardship as a promotional device. When consent or safety is uncertain, pause publication and ask an authorized reviewer.

## 7. Portal and Workspace Guide

### Before your first login

Use the official invitation or activation route sent to you. Confirm that the web address is the official HMSI portal, and do not share your password or one-time setup link. If your invitation contains an HMSI ID, keep it private and use it only for the authorized login process.

### After login

Your portal should take you to the workspace associated with your role. Review your profile, confirm your name and contact details, check the relevant room, and open **My Tasks** or the role-specific work area. Your navigation should show only the tools relevant to your participation pathway.

### Working with task cards

A task card should explain the originating request or dispatch, the action item, the required outcome, priority, due date where applicable, and proof requirements. Use the status sequence provided by the portal, such as **Accept**, **In Progress**, and **Submit Proof / Complete**. Do not mark work complete before the required outcome and proof have been submitted.

### Rooms

Role-based rooms are intended for relevant collaboration and daily activities. Keep messages respectful, factual, and appropriate for the room. Do not forward room content outside authorized channels. If you need another role’s support, use the approved assignment or escalation path rather than posting sensitive information in the wrong room.

### News and publisher submissions

Approved publisher-role participants may use the submission workspace to draft a title, excerpt, body, category, attribution, and media or Drive link. Selecting **Submit for Review** sends the dispatch to the HMSI editorial team. A submitted item is not a published item. Only authorized administrators can approve and publish public content.

## 8. Communication Standards

Use clear subject lines, complete sentences, and a respectful tone. Include the task or programme reference when one exists. Distinguish between an observation, a request, a confirmed fact, an assumption, and an urgent safety concern.

| Situation | Recommended communication |
|---|---|
| You need clarification | “I understand the objective as ____. Please confirm the location, due date, and required proof before I begin.” |
| You are blocked | “Task __ is currently blocked because ____. I have completed ____ and need ____ to continue.” |
| You completed work | “Task __ is complete. Outcome: ____. Proof link: ____. Outstanding follow-up: ____.” |
| You observed an unverified report | “This is an unverified field observation submitted for review; it should not be treated as an official HMSI statement yet.” |
| You have a safeguarding concern | Use the designated confidential reporting route and share only the minimum necessary information. |
| You suspect an account or permission problem | Stop the action and report the route, role, and error without sharing passwords or tokens. |

Avoid sarcasm, humiliating language, political campaigning in official channels, unsupported accusations, and messages that pressure people to reveal private information.

## 9. First-Week Onboarding Plan

### Day 1: Understand the foundation

Read this guide, review the name meaning, mission, vision, values, and role boundaries. Confirm your assigned contact person and identify the official portal address and support route.

### Day 2: Complete your profile

Confirm your display name, email, phone or WhatsApp contact where required, location, primary skill, and role information. Report any incorrect identity or role detail before accepting sensitive work.

### Day 3: Learn the workspace

Sign in, review the dashboard, open your role-based room, locate the task feed, and read the status and proof instructions. Do not test destructive or administrative actions unless specifically authorized.

### Day 4: Review safe participation

Read the safeguarding, privacy, media-consent, and communication guidance. Ask what information must remain confidential and how to report a concern.

### Day 5: Complete a supervised first task

Accept a suitable task or approved learning activity. Confirm the objective, complete the work safely, submit the required update or proof, and request feedback if the expected outcome is unclear.

## 10. Practical Do and Do Not Guide

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Walk alongside people and listen to local knowledge. | Speak about communities as if they have no agency or voice. |
| Describe what is verified and label what is still under review. | Present rumours, drafts, or personal opinions as official HMSI facts. |
| Use approved channels and submit accurate task proof. | Share credentials, private links, or sensitive records in public spaces. |
| Ask for help when a task, permission, or safety condition is unclear. | Work around role controls or use another person’s account. |
| Represent people with dignity and appropriate consent. | Publish identifiable images or stories without approved permission. |
| Update task status honestly and on time. | Mark work complete when the required outcome is not complete. |
| Credit contributors accurately. | Claim another person’s work or imply unauthorized HMSI endorsement. |
| Protect HMSI resources and donor trust. | Promise funds, jobs, services, or outcomes without authorization. |

## 11. Support and Escalation

Use the designated HMSI administrator or team lead for role, assignment, portal, or account questions. Use the safeguarding route for concerns involving safety, abuse, exploitation, harassment, or vulnerable people. Use the security or administrator route for suspected unauthorized access, exposed credentials, incorrect permissions, or suspicious links.

When escalating an issue, provide the smallest useful set of details: your role, the relevant route or task reference, the approximate time, what you expected, what happened, and any safe reproduction steps. Do not include passwords, access tokens, full reset links, or unnecessary personal data.

If an issue affects immediate safety, stop the activity and seek appropriate emergency or local support before continuing with portal administration.
